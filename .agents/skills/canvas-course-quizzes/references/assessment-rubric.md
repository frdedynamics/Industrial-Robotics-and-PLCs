# Assessment Review Rubric

Use this rubric after drafting and before generating QTI. A serious failure in
any required criterion blocks the build even if the JSON is structurally valid.

## Quiz-Level Review

1. **Purpose:** The quiz has a stated formative purpose and a defined source.
2. **Coverage:** Questions sample the page's most useful learning objectives,
   not simply the easiest statements to turn into multiple choice.
3. **Relevance:** The selected knowledge supports later course work, the
   project, or realistic industrial decisions.
4. **Connections:** Relevant relationships to robotics, PLCs, simulation,
   programming, or safety are assessed where they clarify the course as a
   connected whole. Connections are not forced into unrelated items.
5. **Demand:** The set includes understanding and application or diagnosis.
   Pure recall is limited to terminology that learners genuinely need.
6. **Economy:** Two questions do not test essentially the same distinction, and
   no item exists only to increase the question count.
7. **Formative use:** Attempts and feedback timing let learners act on the
   explanation, or there is a stated reason why only one attempt is suitable.
8. **Format mix:** Question types fit their objectives. The quiz varies formats
   where that improves engagement or lets learners demonstrate a different kind
   of understanding, without forcing artificial variety.
9. **Scope and answerability:** The quiz states reasonable assumed
   prerequisites. Its questions can be answered mainly by applying concepts
   taught on learner-visible course pages, with support from explicitly assigned
   resources and those prerequisites where appropriate.

## Item-Level Review

### Objective and prompt

- The objective describes observable understanding: distinguish, select,
  interpret, diagnose, predict, or apply.
- The prompt supplies the context needed for the intended answer.
- A knowledgeable practitioner would identify one clearly best answer without
  guessing what the author meant.
- The wording is concise and accessible to learners using English as an
  additional language.

### Choices

- Every distractor is plausible to a learner with a specific misconception.
- Distractors diagnose errors such as reversing client and server roles,
  confusing internal and external signals, omitting a necessary step, or
  overestimating what a simulation proves.
- Choices are mutually exclusive and comparable in length and specificity.
- The correct answer is not signalled by grammar, precision, or professional
  tone while the distractors are careless or absurd.
- Avoid `all of the above`, `none of the above`, joke answers, and invented
  product behavior.

### Response format

- Single choice asks for one clearly best answer.
- Multiple answer is used only when selecting several independent correct
  statements is part of the intended reasoning. The prompt makes the selection
  rule explicit, and full-credit scoring requires all correct choices and no
  incorrect choices unless another scoring policy is deliberately specified.
- True/false addresses a meaningful claim or common misconception and is not a
  vague statement made false by one minor exception.
- A simpler format is preferred when another type would only add interface work
  without assessing a different capability.

### Feedback

- Correct feedback states why the answer is right and reinforces the transferable
  principle.
- Incorrect feedback explains the distinction that likely caused the error.
- Feedback adds learning value rather than repeating `correct` or `incorrect`.
- Feedback does not depend on answer positions because Canvas may shuffle them.

### Evidence and correctness

- The source material teaches enough for the learner to reason to the correct
  answer; it need not contain the answer as a direct quote.
- The same learner-visible material and stated prerequisites provide a sound
  reason to reject every distractor.
- Required knowledge comes from the target page, relevant linked course pages,
  explicitly assigned resources, or reasonable declared prerequisites.
- The item does not depend on private notes, hidden instructor solutions,
  unassigned documentation, undocumented vendor behavior, or unstated expert
  knowledge. Such sources may verify accuracy but cannot supply missing learner
  context.
- Current software and vendor-specific claims are checked against primary
  documentation when necessary.
- A question clearly marks course-specific architecture or conventions instead
  of presenting them as universal industrial practice.

## Verdicts

- **Keep:** Important objective, sound prompt, diagnostic choices, useful
  feedback, and no unresolved technical concern.
- **Revise:** Worthwhile objective, but the prompt, choices, scope, connection,
  or feedback weakens the assessment.
- **Remove:** Trivia, duplicated coverage, answerable without relevant
  understanding, unsupported claim, or no plausible distractors.
